HAND ERC

Redesigned an open-source robotic hand as a test platform for novel tactile sensors.

Year: 2024–Present Status: Ongoing

Overview

“Robot hands are the interface that transform modern AI to useful physical work.”

HAND ERC is a Northwestern research center focused on developing versatile, dexterous robot hands. As an undergraduate research assistant, I supported the development of novel tactile hand sensors by modifying and building an open-source robotic hand as a test platform (Fig. 1).

Fig. 1: Redesigned Nano Hand in motion

After researching existing open-source robotic hands, I selected the Robot Nano Hand as the platform for redesign and testing. To increase sensor surface area, I used Fusion 360 to fill all gaps in the palm and reposition the thumb connection and motor housing (Fig. 2). These changes required further redesigns to remove interfering geometry and resolve clearance issues (Fig. 3). I then 3D-printed the parts and fully assembled the redesigned Nano Hand.

Fig. 2: CAD changes to the Nano Hand

Fig. 3: Clearance issue example during redesign

Next, I began designing a 3D-printed silicone mold to cast continuous, hollow silicone skin around the Nano Hand. The skin is intended to reduce mechanical noise picked up by the sensors from the roughness of the hand’s PLA surfaces. I first used Blender to model the organic skin geometry and mold core (Fig. 4, 5), then exported the models to Fusion 360 and used Boolean subtraction to create the mold. The complex core geometry created clearance issues during assembly, requiring me to divide the mold into multiple pieces (Fig. 6).

Fig. 4: Silicone skin geometry in Blender

Fig. 5: Mold core in Blender

Fig. 6: Silicone mold CAD, exploded view

Additionally, I redesigned the finger linkages to route the tendons lower to prevent contact with the silicone skin during actuation (Fig. 7).
