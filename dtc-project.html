The Tele

Designed a one-handed diaper-changing station for patients with hemiplegia.

Year: 2024 Status: Complete

Overview

Hemiplegia describes the partial or complete paralysis of one side of the body, which makes two-handed tasks much more difficult.

Mrs. Brianne Trainor, a physical therapist at the Ascension Alexian Brothers Rehabilitation Hospital, was looking for a way to help her patients with hemiplegia be able to change a baby's diaper, a normally two-handed task. Such a solution would give them greater independence when taking care of their baby.

In the first Design Thinking & Communications (DTC) course, I worked with a team of 4 to design a lightweight, one-hand-friendly diaper-changing assistor. Our solution, “The Tele”, is a portable changing station with a metal telescoping mechanism that securely lifts the baby's legs during diapering.

Design Process

I led secondary research of our users and the diaper changing process, allowing us to define initial design and safety requirements. We also conducted research on existing diaper-changing products for design ideation. Then, we interviewed our client to define their specific needs. The three primary requirements were:

  • Ease of use with one hand
  • Accessibility to diaper area
  • Portability

From the start, we planned to have a leg-lifting mechanism that replicated the range of a second hand during diaper changing. Throughout the project, we would build mockups in the shop and bring them to the hospital for user testing (Fig. 1).

For our final prototype, I proposed a widened luggage handle as the lifting mechanism, which would allow for stable and variable height adjustment at the press of a button and is durable enough to withstand baby kicks. See Fig. 2 for an exploded view I drew of the luggage mechanism. I led the design of the widened luggage handlebar in Onshape CAD (Fig. 3), which we then 3D-printed. In the shop, we used the bandsaw to cut an existing luggage handle to size and drilled additional holes for variable height adjustment. We selected high density polyethylene plastic as the base material against traditional PVC plastic due to medical concerns over PVC. Finally, we connected the base and luggage mechanism with a pair of locking hinges and screws.

We delivered our final prototype to our client, along with a technical report detailing the design process, instructions for construction, and instructions for use. Additionally, we presented our design to students and faculty at the DTC expo (Fig. 4). See Fig. 5 for how a baby would use this mechanism.
