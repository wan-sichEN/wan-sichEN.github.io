Robotic Band

Built Pulse, a six-armed robot drummer, with Northwestern’s Robotics Club.

Year: 2024–Present Status: Ongoing

Overview

At the Northwestern University Robotics Club (NURC), I became an early member of the Robotic Band subteam, the perfect environment to pursue both my love for music and robots.

In 2024, I began working on the band’s robot drummer, Pulse, focusing on CAD and 3D-printing components for the arms and torso. Pulse has 6 arms, 1 per drum, with servo motors and linkages to swing its drumsticks. 3 stepper motors control the torso’s pitch and yaw movement, allowing the robot to bob up and down to its own music. Its head was designed like an old beige box computer and can display faces and videos during performances. See Fig. 1 for a video of Pulse in action.

Fig. 1: Pulse in action

At the end of my freshman year, Pulse gave its first performance at Northwestern’s 2025 Kresgepalooza music event. Alongside human musicians, it performed several Daft Punk songs, including “Get Lucky” (Fig. 2). I designed the sheet metal torso shown in the video with Onshape’s bend simulation (Fig. 3, 4). It remains one of my favorite contributions.

During my sophomore year, I continued working on Pulse to improve torso stability for performances. With the Drummer Mechanical Team, I helped ideate stabilizing mechanisms, including gear boxes for pitch and a turntable for yaw motion, and performed hand-calculations to analyze torso loads and select gear ratios. I then designed new actuation components and performed hand-calculated stress analysis for verification and material selection. See Fig. 5 and Fig. 6 for a before and after of the actuation design. I also redesigned weak, easily broken arm joints with additional fillets, ribs, and more uniform cross-sections, and had them printed at higher infills to improve strength (Fig. 7).

With these improvements, Pulse put on quite a show as it jammed and grooved at Northwestern’s 2026 Kresgepalooza. This time, alongside members of Robotic Band (including me as the band’s bassist), it performed several pop culture hits like “Mr. Brightside” by The Killers (Fig. 8).
